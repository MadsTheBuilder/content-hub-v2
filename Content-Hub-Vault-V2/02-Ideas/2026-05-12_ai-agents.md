# Video Ideas — AI agents

---

### Rank 1 — The LLM as a "Shitty Computer": How to Engineer Reliability into AI Agents

**Angle:** Deconstructs the LLM from a magical black box into a predictable (but unreliable) computer, demonstrating how applying traditional software engineering principles makes it reliable.

**Gap it fills:** Most content treats LLMs as conversational AI or as "magic," failing to equip developers with the engineering mindset needed to build robust, deterministic systems around their inherent unreliability.

**Audience desire:** "I need to build something that actually works consistently with LLMs, not just a fancy demo. How do I make these things reliable?"

**Competitive signal:** The research notes highlight this as "a primary shift in engineering mindset," implying a foundational concept that is under-explained but critical for practical application.

**Hook direction:** "Forget what you thought you knew about LLMs. If you want to build anything serious, you need to start seeing them as the worst computer you've ever used – and then engineer around it."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8-15min. This topic requires a conceptual shift, examples, and detailed explanation of how traditional SE principles apply (memory, I/O, state).

### Rank 2 — Minimalist Agent Design: You Only Need 4 Tools for Production-Ready AI

**Angle:** Challenges the "feature bloat" trend by demonstrating how to build robust, stable, and observable AI agents using only four core tools, as championed by the "pi" philosophy.

**Gap it fills:** Counteracts the common instinct to add more tools and complexity to agents, offering a clear, actionable path to simplification and stability often missing in agent-building tutorials.

**Audience desire:** "I'm overwhelmed by all the agent frameworks and tools. How do I build a production-ready agent without it becoming a black-box mess?"

**Competitive signal:** The "Minimalist Agent Design Principles" explicitly list the four tools and critique "feature bloat," indicating a strong, opinionated angle with a proven track record (the "pi" agent).

**Hook direction:** "Most people are building AI agents all wrong. You're probably using too many tools. Here's why you only need four core functions to build a truly stable, production-ready AI."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8-15min. A practical guide requiring demonstration of each tool's function and how they combine for complex tasks.

### Rank 3 — Stop Context Rot: Surgical Updates for Production LLM Agents (feat. `jq`)

**Angle:** A deep dive into concrete strategies for combating "context degradation" in long-running agent sessions, focusing on token-efficient "surgical updates" using tools like `jq`.

**Gap it fills:** While context window limits are discussed, practical, production-grade solutions for managing context degradation and persistent state over extended agent operations are rarely shown in detail.

**Audience desire:** "My AI agent keeps losing its focus and forgetting critical details after a while. How do I make it maintain its state and remember important information efficiently?"

**Competitive signal:** The research explicitly defines "Context Rot" at 100k tokens and highlights `jq` for "surgical updates" to JSON state, offering a specific, technical solution to a common pain point.

**Hook direction:** "Your LLM agent is forgetting things, and it's costing you money. It's called 'context rot,' and if you're not surgically updating your agent's memory, you're losing performance and tokens."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8-15min. Requires code examples and demonstrations of `jq` for state management and strategies for progressive disclosure.

**Angle:** A data-backed, direct comparison of agentic architectures (CLI vs. MCP), revealing why CLI tools are often significantly more cost-effective and performant for complex agent tasks.

**Gap it fills:** Many agent framework tutorials gloss over the architectural choice for tool integration, or default to less efficient options. This video provides a clear, data-driven rationale for making this critical decision.

**Audience desire:** "Which agent architecture should I pick to get the best performance and cost efficiency? I need concrete numbers, not just theory."

**Competitive signal:** The research explicitly states, "well-designed CLI tools can be 39% more cost-effective than standard multiplexers" and details the trade-offs, providing strong quantitative evidence.

**Hook direction:** "You're probably picking the wrong architecture for your AI agent. Stop wasting 39% of your budget and slowing down your agent. Here's when to use CLI tools over Model Context Protocol."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8-15min. An analytical video presenting benchmark data, architectural diagrams, and practical examples for both approaches.

### Rank 7 — Your Agent's External Brain: Using `TODO.md` for Persistent Planning

**Angle:** Demonstrates a simple, highly effective method for preventing context degradation in agent planning by forcing the agent to maintain its plan in an external, version-controlled `TODO.md` file.

**Gap it fills:** Many agents struggle with maintaining long-term plans or making their internal planning transparent. This provides a minimalist, robust solution that bypasses context window limitations.

**Audience desire:** "My AI agent keeps losing its train of thought or going off-script. How do I make it consistently follow a plan and track its progress reliably?"

**Competitive signal:** The "Minimalist Agent Design Principles" explicitly state, "Avoid built-in 'Plan Modes.' Instead, force the agent to maintain a `TODO.md` or `PLAN.md` file," highlighting this as a core, proven technique.

**Hook direction:** "Your AI agent's biggest problem? It forgets its own plan. The solution isn't more context or complex internal modes; it's a simple `TODO.md` file. Here's why it works."

**Format recommendation:** Both. A short-form video (60-90s) to introduce the concept, followed by a long-form (8-15min) tutorial demonstrating implementation and benefits.

### Rank 8 — Beyond "Spaceship Prompts": The 1000-Token Rule for AI Agent Prompts

**Angle:** Explores the counter-intuitive finding that minimalist system prompts (under 1000 tokens) are often superior to verbose "spaceship" prompts for AI agents, reducing confusion and cost.

**Gap it fills:** Challenges the common belief that "more detailed prompts are always better." This provides data-backed evidence for concise, targeted prompt design specifically for agentic systems.

**Audience desire:** "My prompts are getting huge, but my agent is still confused. How do I write more effective, cheaper prompts that actually make my agent smarter?"

**Competitive signal:** Research states, "Effective agents... demonstrate that a system prompt under 1,000 tokens is often superior to 'spaceship' prompts of 10,000+ tokens, which often confuse the model."

**Hook direction:** "Stop writing giant prompts for your AI agents. Less is more, and I'll show you how a prompt under 1000 tokens will outperform your 10,000-token 'spaceship' every single time."

**Format recommendation:** Both. A short-form video (60-90s) to grab attention with the core finding, and a long-form (8-15min) video with examples of effective minimalist prompts and their impact.

### Rank 9 — Stop Guessing: Co-Debugging Your AI Agent with Real-Time Observability

**Angle:** Teaches developers how to implement robust observability patterns (like inspecting raw ANSI streams or attaching to `tmux` sessions) to effectively "co-debug" AI agents, avoiding opaque "black box" sub-agents.

**Gap it fills:** Most agent frameworks abstract away the execution, making debugging challenging. This focuses on transparency and direct human insight into the agent's thought process and actions.

**Audience desire:** "My AI agent broke, and I have no idea why. How do I see inside its brain and understand what it's *actually* doing in real-time to fix it?"

**Competitive signal:** The research notes clearly critique "Sub-agents are often 'black boxes within black boxes'" and advocate for spawning processes via Bash or `tmux` for direct human inspection and "co-debugging."

**Hook direction:** "If your AI agent breaks, you're probably blind to *why*. Stop using black box sub-agents. I'll show you how to 'co-debug' your AI as if it's sitting right next to you, watching its every move."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8-15min. A tutorial demonstrating how to set up and use these observability techniques with a simple agent example.
