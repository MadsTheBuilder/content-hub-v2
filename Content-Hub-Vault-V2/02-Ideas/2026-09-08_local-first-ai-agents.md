# Video Ideas — local-first AI agents

Flagging one thing before the list: the topic field says "local-first AI agents" but the research notes are entirely AI-for-real-estate. The notes are the only real substrate here, so I built on those — say the word if you actually wanted local-first agent ideas and I'll re-run against a matching brief.

---

### Rank 1 — ChatGPT Only Names 5 Agents In Your City. Here's How To Be One Of Them.

**Angle:** Treats AI recommendation as a fixed-inventory competition — five slots, one city — instead of a vague "optimize for AI" lecture.

**Gap it fills:** Every existing video frames AI as a tool the agent uses. Nobody has framed AI as the referral source that's already picking agents without you. The notes say fewer than 1% of agents are optimized for this, which means no one has made the practical version of this video either.

**Audience desire:** "Am I already invisible, and how do I check right now?" They search "AI for realtors," but the fear is being replaced in the recommendation layer, not in the writing layer.

**Competitive signal:** 67% of buyers say they're comfortable letting ChatGPT choose their agent, Google AI Overview returns exactly one name, and Zillow shipped inside ChatGPT in 2025 with Redfin following within weeks. That's a live distribution shift with almost no content covering it.

**Hook direction:** Open by typing "best realtor in [your city]" into ChatGPT on screen, reading the five names out loud, and saying "one of these is worse than you and it doesn't matter."

**Format recommendation:** Both — long-form 10–14min for the full four-signal method (Google Business Profile, transcripts, social, reviews), 60s short of just the live query and the five names, which is the entire hook by itself.

### Rank 2 — 88% Of Agents Use AI For Listings. 17% Get Results. The Difference Is One Paragraph.

**Angle:** Diagnoses the failure as input specificity and proves it with the same property run twice — vague prompt vs. loaded prompt — side by side on screen.

**Gap it fills:** Existing videos hand out prompts. None of them show the bad output next to the good one, so viewers copy a template, get mush, and quietly conclude AI doesn't work for them.

**Audience desire:** "I already tried this and the output was garbage — was that AI's fault or mine?" They won't ask it out loud because the answer is embarrassing.

**Competitive signal:** The 88/17 split is straight from the research and it's the single most damning stat in the whole set: near-universal adoption, near-total failure. That gap is the video.

**Hook direction:** Show the generic AI listing description first, let it sit for three seconds, then say "88% of agents ship this. Here's the exact 60 words that were missing."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8–12min — the value is the A/B comparison, which needs screen time to land. Shorts of this get read as another prompt-dump.

### Rank 3 — I Let ChatGPT Play The Angriest Expired Seller In My City. It Destroyed Me.

**Angle:** You're the one who fails on camera — the roleplay is real, unscripted, and you lose the first call before you win the third.

**Gap it fills:** The notes describe ChatGPT-as-homeowner as a capability but nobody has filmed the actual thing. Coaching content shows polished agents delivering clean scripts, which teaches nothing because the objection never lands hard.

**Audience desire:** "What happens when they get hostile and my script runs out?" That's the reason agents don't dial expireds — not skill, rehearsal.

**Competitive signal:** Agents were paying $5,000–6,000 for coaching programs to get exactly these reps. A free, infinite, market-specific replacement for a $6k product is a strong click, and expired-listing content already has proven pull in this niche.

**Hook direction:** Cold open on the AI's line — "I already had one of you people for six months and you did nothing" — then cut to you saying "that's ChatGPT, and it's about to run me through this eleven more times."

**Format recommendation:** Both — long-form 12–15min for three full call attempts showing the improvement curve, plus a 60s short of the single most brutal objection and your fixed response.

### Rank 4 — Your Leads Die In 5 Minutes. Here's The Thing That Answers At 2AM.

**Angle:** Builds the responder live end to end — trigger, qualifying questions, handoff rule — rather than citing speed-to-lead as a statistic and moving on.

**Gap it fills:** "Respond in five minutes" is repeated everywhere as advice. Nobody shows the machine that does it while you sleep, or the exact point where it stops and pings a human.

**Audience desire:** "What does this actually cost me, and will it embarrass me in front of a lead?" The unspoken blocker is fear of a bot saying something stupid to a $2M buyer.

**Competitive signal:** The notes tie the whole ROI story to this window — 3× leads on 60–80% fewer hours, 28% more transactions year over year per NAR. The stat is everywhere; the build is nowhere.

**Hook direction:** Send a fake lead form at 2:47am on screen, show the reply timestamp, then say "I was asleep. That took nine seconds and it costs less than one Zillow lead."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 12–15min — this is a build video and the handoff logic is the part people need to see. Seeds follow-ups on qualification questions and CRM wiring.

### Rank 5 — AI Can't Recommend You If It Can't Categorize You (I'll Rewrite Your Bio Live)

**Angle:** Attacks the single sentence — the positioning line — as the atomic unit AI uses to file you, and rewrites viewer-submitted bios on camera.

**Gap it fills:** Niche-down advice is ancient and ignored because it's framed as marketing philosophy. Reframing it as a machine-readability problem gives it a hard, testable reason that "stand out" never had.

**Audience desire:** "Do I have to give up half my business to niche down?" They resist because it reads as turning away clients, not as becoming indexable.

**Competitive signal:** The notes are blunt about it — "I help first-time buyers in Austin navigate the market stress-free" is categorizable, "I help buyers and sellers" is nobody. That's a before/after you can run twenty times in one video.

**Hook direction:** Read three real agent bios in a flat voice, then ask ChatGPT to describe each one's specialty and let it fail on all three.

**Format recommendation:** Both — long-form 8–10min for the rewrites, and this is the best short-form engine in the list: one bio, one rewrite, 45 seconds, infinitely repeatable as a series.

### Rank 6 — I Stopped Making YouTube Videos For Buyers. I Make Them For ChatGPT Now.

**Angle:** Reframes video as transcript production for an indexer, with the script written to be quoted by a model rather than watched by a human.

**Gap it fills:** Realtor-YouTube advice is all thumbnails, retention, and views. Nothing addresses the fact that the transcript outlives the view count and gets scraped as a citation.

**Audience desire:** "Is YouTube worth it if my videos only get 200 views?" The answer nobody has given them is that 200 views and a well-structured transcript can still get you named.

**Competitive signal:** A creator in the source set verified ChatGPT appearing in his YouTube analytics as an actual traffic source. That's a screenshot-able, falsifiable claim and it's the whole proof of the thesis.

**Hook direction:** Open on the analytics screen with ChatGPT listed as a traffic source and say "this line didn't exist eighteen months ago and almost nobody has noticed it."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8–12min — needs the analytics proof plus the transcript-structuring method (stating city, specialty, and question-form headings out loud).

### Rank 7 — One Afternoon, One Relocation Guide, 7 Leads In Week One

**Angle:** A real-time build with the clock running — guide, lead magnet video, follow-up sequence — ending with the actual lead count instead of a promise.

**Gap it fills:** Lead magnet content stops at "make a guide." It skips the distribution and the follow-up, which is where the leads actually come from and where everyone stalls.

**Audience desire:** "How long until this produces something, realistically?" They've been burned by systems that take three months to show a signal.

**Competitive signal:** The source case is unusually complete for this niche — ChatGPT plus Gamma, built in one afternoon, 7 leads in week one, 2 buyers closed within 45 days. Specific numbers with a short time-to-proof convert.

**Hook direction:** Start the timer on screen at 0:00 and say "I have until my kid's pickup to build a lead magnet that works. Everything after this is unedited."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 10–15min — the build-in-real-time format is the credibility, and cutting it destroys the point.

### Rank 8 — The Fair Housing Landmine Hiding In Your AI Listing Description

**Angle:** Runs AI-generated listing copy against the actual prohibited-language patterns and shows which phrases it produces unprompted.

**Gap it fills:** Every listing-automation video celebrates the speed. Not one addresses that the model happily writes "perfect for a young family" or "safe neighborhood" and you're the one who published it.

**Audience desire:** "What's the thing that bites me later?" Compliance anxiety is the strongest unspoken brake on adoption for licensed agents, and nobody is speaking to it.

**Competitive signal:** 88% of realtors are already using AI for listings per the notes — meaning the exposed population is nearly everyone, and the search intent already exists under a different name.

**Hook direction:** Show a clean-looking AI-written description, highlight four phrases in red, and say "three of these are the ones brokerages get letters about."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 8–10min — you need to show the generation, the flagged phrases, and the corrective prompt line that suppresses them. Keep it descriptive of the language patterns and point viewers to their broker for anything case-specific.

### Rank 9 — "10–15 Seller Leads A Week On Autopilot" — I Reverse-Engineered What That Funnel Actually Is

**Angle:** Takes a claim everyone repeats and diagrams the machine underneath it — traffic source, offer, qualifier, nurture cadence — instead of restating the outcome.

**Gap it fills:** Autopilot funnel claims circulate as results with the mechanism deliberately vague. A structural teardown is the video the claim keeps generating demand for and never satisfies.

**Audience desire:** "Is this real or is it a coaching upsell?" They want the architecture so they can judge whether it's replicable at their volume.

**Competitive signal:** Seller leads are the highest-value keyword in the niche, and the notes cite the 10–15/week figure without explaining the build — a stated outcome with a missing middle is the clearest content gap shape there is.

**Hook direction:** Say the claim out loud, pause, then "here's what has to be true for that number to work, and one of these four pieces is where most people quit."

**Format recommendation:** Long-form 10–14min — needs a whiteboard or diagram walkthrough. Strong series anchor: each of the four components becomes its own build video.
