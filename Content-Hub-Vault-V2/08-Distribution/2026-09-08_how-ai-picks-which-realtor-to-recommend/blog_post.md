Twelve thousand realtors in one city. No ranking, no benchmark, nobody official saying who's best. Ask an AI and it still hands you one name, every time, no hesitation. That same call is being made about your product right now, in someone else's chat window.

## Ten results became one name

Search gave you ten blue links. Ten links meant ten businesses had a shot at the click. AI gives you one name, so one business gets it and the other nine never made it into the sentence.

There's no page two to sit on anymore. You're the name in the answer or you're the twelve thousand. Middle of the pack used to still pull traffic. Now it pulls nothing.

i picked realtors because they're the cleanest test i could find. No leaderboard, no benchmark, and star ratings parked at 4.8 across the whole market, so there's nothing real to rank on. Whatever the model uses instead has nowhere to hide. And that same thing is running in your category too.

## Three obvious theories, all wrong

**Reviews.** Most reviews, most trust, most recommendations. i pulled review counts for every name the model gave me and every name it skipped. The most-recommended agent has a double-digit review count. Agents sitting on four figures never came up once.

**Sales volume.** The actual top producers. This one reframes the whole problem, and the reason is simple — those numbers live inside MLS portals and brokerage dashboards, behind a login. The model has never seen a single row. Being genuinely the best sits somewhere it physically cannot read, so it can't be what's getting scored.

**Website quality.** One of the three winners has a one-page site. Headshot, phone number, that's it. Agents with full IDX search, weekly blogs and video libraries never got named.

## Retrieval gets you in the room. It doesn't get you named.

When you ask, the assistant doesn't answer from memory. It runs a search first. That search comes back with maybe eight to twelve pages, and everything it says next comes out of those pages. Nothing else is in the room.

The obvious place to land here is "so it's SEO — rank number one, get retrieved, get recommended." That's where most builders are putting their money. i checked. The page ranking number one for "best realtor" in my city gets retrieved on almost every run, and the model still won't name the agent sitting at the top of it.

Something happens *after* retrieval that throws most of that page out. That step is where everyone is losing.

## The model isn't looking anything up

There is no database of best realtors. The model isn't querying one. It's building a name out of sentences other people wrote.

So the three theories were always going to fail. Review counts, sales volume, site quality — those are attributes of *you*. What actually gets said out loud is whatever exists as a clean, attributable, reusable sentence on a page someone else published.

Forty prompts. Four phrasings, ten runs each, fresh session every time, no memory, no personalization. Three names carry the entire set. By run twelve you can call it before it finishes typing.

The winners weren't the best agents. They were the ones written about in a shape the model could lift.

## What This Means

Stop pouring money into the thing the model can't see. Go audit what third-party pages actually say about you, in sentences, by name — directories, roundups, local press, forum threads, partner pages. If your differentiator only lives on your own site or behind a login, it doesn't exist. Getting retrieved is table stakes. Getting quoted is the job.
