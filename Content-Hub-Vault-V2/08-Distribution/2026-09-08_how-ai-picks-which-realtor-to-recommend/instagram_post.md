# Instagram Carousel

**Slide 1:** Twelve thousand realtors. No public ranking anywhere. The AI still picks one — confidently, every single time.

**Slide 2:** Search used to hand you ten blue links. Ten businesses got a shot at that click.

AI gives you one name. The other nine were never even in the sentence.

**Slide 3:** Theory 1: it picks whoever has the most reviews.

Nope. The most-recommended agent had a double-digit review count. Agents sitting on 1,000+ reviews never showed up once.

**Slide 4:** Theory 2: it picks the top producers.

Also nope. Sales volume lives inside MLS portals and brokerage dashboards, behind a login.

So being genuinely the best is stored somewhere the model physically cannot read.

**Slide 5:** Theory 3: it picks the best website.

One of the three winners has a single-page site. Headshot. Phone number. That's it.

Agents with full IDX search, weekly blogs and entire video libraries were never named.

**Slide 6:** Here's what's actually going on.

The model fires a search, pulls back 8–12 pages, and everything it says after that comes out of those pages. Nothing else is in the room.

It isn't looking up "best realtor" in some database. That database doesn't exist. It's building a name out of sentences other people wrote.

**Slide 7:** And ranking #1 isn't enough. The top-ranked page gets pulled on almost every run — and the model still doesn't name the agent sitting at the top of it.

Retrieval gets you in the room. It does not get you named.

**Slide 8:** 40 prompts. 4 phrasings. 10 runs each. Fresh session every time, no memory, no personalization.

Three names carried the whole thing.
