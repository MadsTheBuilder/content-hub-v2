# LinkedIn Post

AI doesn't hand you ten links anymore. It hands you one name.

I ran 40 prompts asking for a realtor recommendation in one city — four phrasings, ten runs each, fresh session every time. Twelve thousand agents in that market. Three names carried the whole thing.

Here's what didn't explain it:

→ **Reviews.** The most-recommended agent had a double-digit review count. Agents sitting on four figures never showed up once.

→ **Sales volume.** The actual top producers live inside MLS portals and brokerage dashboards, behind a login. The model has never seen a single row of it. So being genuinely the best is sitting somewhere it physically can't read.

→ **Website quality.** One of the winners has a single-page site. Headshot, phone number, that's it. Agents with full IDX search and video libraries never got named.

→ **SEO.** The page ranking #1 for "best realtor" gets pulled in on almost every run, and the model still doesn't name the agent at the top of it.

That last one is the part worth sitting with. Retrieval gets you into the room. It doesn't get you named. Something happens *after* retrieval that throws most of the page away.

The model isn't querying a database of best realtors. That database doesn't exist. It's assembling a name out of sentences other people wrote.

Realtors were just a clean test case — no leaderboard, no benchmark, ratings flat at 4.8 across the market. Whatever's doing the work there is doing it in your category too.

Have you checked whether AI names your product when someone asks for one like it? What came back?
