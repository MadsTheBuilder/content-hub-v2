# Twitter/X Thread

---

**Tweet 1 (hook)**
Your coding agent is already a UI designer. You just never gave it the brief.

---

**Tweet 2**
Every builder treats their agent like a backend dev: write the logic, wire the tools, ship it. Then spends 3 days fighting the frontend.

The agent isn't the problem. The prompt is.

---

**Tweet 3**
Most people try Tailwind after the fact, bolt on a component library, or ask the agent mid-session to "make it look better."

All three fail. The agent has no design context to pull from.

---

**Tweet 4**
My first instinct: write a 200-line design spec. Color palette. Spacing tokens. Component names.

The output looked exactly the same.

---

**Tweet 5**
The builders who consistently ship polished AI apps don't write design documents.

They do something that takes 15 seconds. First 3 lines, before any feature is described. Here's what they are:

---

**Tweet 6**
Line 1 — Role:

"You are a senior product designer and front-end engineer. Output should feel production-ready, not prototype-ready."

Not flattery. Context selection. The agent leads with design evaluation before any feature is described.

---

**Tweet 7**
Line 2 — Register:

"This app is for [audience]. It should feel like [Linear/Stripe/Vercel] — clean, trustworthy, minimal friction."

One sentence. Your agent has seen these products thousands of times. It knows what they look like at the component level.

---

**Tweet 8**
Line 3 — Quality gate:

"Before writing any component, ask: would a senior designer approve this? If not, revise before output."

This installs a self-evaluation loop. You receive something that already cleared a bar — not a first draft.

---

**Tweet 9**
I shipped 4 AI tools in 6 months. First 3: gray, unstyled, unused past the demo.

The 4th? Three separate people asked what design agency I hired.

Same agent. Same stack. Different opening 3 lines.

---

**Tweet 10**
The real unlock isn't about design — it's identity priming.

Give the agent no role → it defaults to neutral. Neutral means functional. Never beautiful.

Assign a role with a quality standard before the job starts, not after you've seen bad output.

---

**Tweet 11**
This applies to every specialist you need: strict code reviewer, skeptical product manager, copywriter who treats jargon as a bug.

The agent can operate in all of those modes. It just needs to know that's the job before the job starts.
