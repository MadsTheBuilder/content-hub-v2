# Hook Variations: how does this help 

### Hook 1 — 6-Power-Word Stack

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "If I had to build a full design pipeline with Claude Code from zero, this is the first repo I'd install."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "It's called Open Design. 19 design skills, 71 brand-grade design systems — and it ships without its own AI. It uses yours. Whatever coding agent you have installed, it finds it and feeds it the work."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'll show you how the architecture works, what a real session looks like in the terminal, and whether the output is actually production-ready or just a demo."

**Visual Cue:**
> Terminal output streaming; hard cut to a polished UI prototype — no design tool visible anywhere in the workflow.

**Text Overlay:**
> `19 skills. Your agent. $0.`

**Kallaway Pattern:** 6-Power-Word Stack
**Why:** "From zero" signals hard-won knowledge — the viewer treats the recommendation as a shortcut to experience they haven't paid for yet.

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "Open Design doesn't bundle its own AI. It detects whatever agent CLI you have on your machine — Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Gemini CLI — and hands it the design loop. Your agent was always capable of this. The layer just didn't exist until now."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'll walk through the architecture, show what detection looks like under the hood, and explain why this collapses the tool stack AI builders have been managing manually."

**Visual Cue:**
> Split screen: left — Figma with $20/month badge; right — terminal running `open-design`. Right side wins. Left fades.

**Text Overlay:**
> `Your agent was capable. The layer just didn't exist.`

**Kallaway Pattern:** Contrast-Driven Curiosity
**Why:** Telling the viewer they already own the capability creates dissonance their brain immediately moves to resolve — the curiosity loop opens before a single explanation is given.

### Hook 3 — Pain Point Agitation

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "Tool-switching between Figma, your coding agent, and three export tools just to ship one prototype? The fragmentation isn't you — it's the architecture."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "Design tooling was never built to talk to coding agents. Every handoff is manual, every export is a separate step, every iteration costs another context switch. That's not inefficiency — that's a structural gap."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "This video breaks down an open-source layer that closes it — runs on the agent you already use, exports HTML, PDF, PPTX, and MP4 from one command."

**Visual Cue:**
> Rapid-fire cuts: Figma tab → export dialog → Claude tab → terminal → Figma again. Then: one terminal. One command. Output.

**Text Overlay:**
> `The fragmentation is structural. This is the fix.`

**Kallaway Pattern:** Pain Point Agitation
**Why:** Naming the exact friction — then reframing it as a structural problem rather than user error — makes the viewer feel validated and motivated to find the fix.

### Hook 5 — Stated Contrast

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "Most AI builders run three separate subscriptions to go from idea to shipped design. This open-source repo replaces all of them — inside the agent you already have — for free."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "Open Design. 71 design systems, 19 skills. Runs on Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, Gemini CLI, or Qwen. Exports web prototypes, desktop UI, slides, and video. Local-first. Sandboxed. No subscription."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'll show you how it hooks into your existing CLI, what the output actually looks like, and whether it's production-ready or just good enough for demos."

**Visual Cue:**
> Three app icons with price tags (Figma $20, Framer $19, Canva $15) slide left. Terminal running `open-design` stays center. Price tag: `$0`.

**Text Overlay:**
> `$54/month → $0. Same output. Your existing agent.`

**Kallaway Pattern:** Stated Contrast
**Why:** Explicit dollar figures trigger loss-aversion immediately — the viewer is already paying for the baseline, so the free alternative lands as a personal saving, not an abstract feature claim.
