# Hook Variations: Your Coding Agent Already Has a UI Designer Built In — You Just Don't Know It

### Hook 1 — Implied Contrast

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "Your coding agent is already a UI designer. You just never gave it the brief."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "Every builder I've watched treats their agent like a backend dev: write the logic, wire the tools, ship it. Then spends three days fighting the frontend because they assume that part requires a different brain entirely. The agent isn't the problem. The prompt is."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'm going to show you the prompt structure that activates your agent's design thinking — a layer that's been sitting there unused — so your next app ships looking like someone who actually cares about UI built it."

**Visual Cue** (what is on screen during the hook):
> Split screen: left is a bare, unstyled AI app; right is the same app with polished UI. Text fades in: "Same agent. Same session."

**Text Overlay:**
> "You Already Have a UI Designer"

**Kallaway Pattern:** Implied Contrast
**Why:** Drops a contrarian claim with zero setup — the viewer's brain immediately asks "wait, how?" — which is the curiosity gap that holds them into the video.

### Hook 2 — Pain Point Agitation ⭐

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "If your AI app works perfectly but looks like it was built by a backend developer — this is why."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "You spent a week on the logic. The tools are wired. The responses are clean. Then you demo it to someone outside your team and watch their face. You already know. It doesn't look like something people trust."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "There's a prompt sequence — three lines — that tells your agent to think like a UI designer before it writes a single component. I'll show you exactly what it looks like and why it works."

**Visual Cue** (what is on screen during the hook):
> Screen recording of a raw, unstyled AI chat interface. Cursor hovers over it. Hard cut to: same interface after one prompt pass — clean, structured, production-ready.

**Text Overlay:**
> "Why Your AI App Looks Like a Dev Tool"

**Kallaway Pattern:** Pain Point Agitation
**Why:** Names the exact humiliation moment AI builders recognize — the demo face-watch — then promises a three-line fix, triggering the "I need this now" reflex.

### Hook 3 — Contrast-Driven Curiosity (The Curiosity Loop)

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "What if the reason your AI apps look like internal tools is a prompting problem — not a design problem?"

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "Not a CSS problem. Not a Figma problem. A prompting problem. Your coding agent has seen more production interfaces than most designers ever will. It knows what good looks like. You just never told it that's the job."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "By the end of this video you'll know exactly why that gap exists — and you'll have the three-word addition to your system prompt that closes it."

**Visual Cue** (what is on screen during the hook):
> Zoom into a prompt file in VS Code. One line highlighted in yellow. Hard cut to: before/after UI comparison playing at 2x speed.

**Text Overlay:**
> "It's Not a Design Problem"

**Kallaway Pattern:** Contrast-Driven Curiosity
**Why:** Reframes a pain the audience already carries (ugly apps) as something surprising and fixable — the unanswered "why is it a prompting problem?" keeps them watching to close the loop.

### Hook 4 — 6-Power-Word Stack

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "If I had to ship a polished AI app from scratch again — in one session — here's what I'd do differently."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "I've shipped four AI tools in the past six months. The first three looked like nobody thought about the user. The fourth one? Three people asked me what design agency I hired. Same coding agent. Different prompts."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'm walking you through the exact session structure: what I ask the agent first, what context I give it, and the one step most builders skip — the difference between a dev tool and a product."

**Visual Cue** (what is on screen during the hook):
> Grid of four app screenshots: first three plain and gray, fourth polished. Timer graphic overlay: "1 session."

**Text Overlay:**
> "Same Agent. Different Prompts."

**Kallaway Pattern:** 6-Power-Word Stack
**Why:** The "again" + "one session" + implicit failure-to-success arc delivers credibility with urgency — the viewer needs to know exactly what changed between attempts three and four.

### Hook 5 — The You/Your Pivot

**Spoken Line** (first 3 seconds — stop the scroll):
> "You've been using your coding agent as a developer. You should've been using it as a design system."

**Setup** (next 10 seconds — deepen the curiosity gap):
> "You give it a task: build this feature, wire this API, write this component. You never tell it who the user is, what the app is supposed to feel like, or what polished looks like in this context. So it gives you functional. Never beautiful."

**Promise** (next 15 seconds — what they get if they keep watching):
> "I'll show you how to add a design layer to any coding agent — no Figma, no CSS expertise, no extra tools. Just a different way to open the session."

**Visual Cue** (what is on screen during the hook):
> Creator typing into a chat interface — prompt visible on screen. App renders in the background: plain first, then restyled after a single follow-up prompt.

**Text Overlay:**
> "Stop Using It Like a Dev. Use It Like a Designer."

**Kallaway Pattern:** The You/Your Pivot
**Why:** Flips the frame from tool capability to user behavior — making the viewer the subject of the mistake triggers an immediate "wait, am I doing this wrong?" that they have to resolve.
