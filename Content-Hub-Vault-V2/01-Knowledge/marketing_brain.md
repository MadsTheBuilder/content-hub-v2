## Hook Formulas

- **The 6-Power-Word Stack**
  - **Structure:** Subject Clarity Word + Action Word + Objective/End-State Word + Contrast Word + (Optional) Proof Word + (Optional) Time Word
  - **Example:** "If I had to grow from 0 to 100k subs on YouTube **again** in **5 months**, here is how I would do it"
  - **When:** Foundational template for any educational or transformation hook needing urgency and credibility

- **Contrast-Driven Curiosity (The Curiosity Loop)**
  - **Structure:** Contrast viewer's current belief (A) against a contrarian alternative (B)
  - **Example:** "This is going to change how accountants do their job"
  - **When:** Awareness Game content; anytime you need to open an unanswered question the viewer must solve

- **Speed to Value (Rapid Context)**
  - **Structure:** Move the topic introduction to the first 1–2 seconds. Zero fluff.
  - **Example:** "Here are three simple ways to improve your gut health" — NOT "Guys, this is the craziest thing..."
  - **When:** Every video — prevents exponential retention decay in the opening seconds

- **The "You/Your" Pivot (Relevance Strategy)**
  - **Structure:** Replace "I/Me" with "You/Your" in the hook
  - **Example:** "If you've struggled with skin problems your whole life..." vs. "I've struggled with skin problems..."
  - **When:** Whenever targeting a specific audience avatar — eliminates the "does this apply to me?" drop-off

- **Stated Contrast (A vs. B)**
  - **Structure:** Explicitly name baseline (A) AND the contrarian alternative (B)
  - **Example:** "Most people solve their acne with Accutane, but I have an herbal remedy that does it three times faster"
  - **When:** Blunt hooks where the advantage over a known solution needs to land hard

- **Implied Contrast**
  - **Structure:** State the contrarian alternative (B) only — assume viewer already knows (A)
  - **Example:** "This herbal supplement is eight times more effective for solving acne"
  - **When:** When the baseline is so well-known that stating it feels redundant

- **The Visual Stun Gun (Level 1 Stimulation)**
  - **Structure:** Vivid colors + high motion + brightness in first 200ms
  - **Example:** Bright orange graphic or sudden zoom-in on a shocked face
  - **When:** Every video opening — earns the split-second needed to deliver the verbal hook

- **Anticipation / The "Edging" Hook**
  - **Structure:** Pose question → provide details that help viewer guess → yank the answer away (head fake) → reset loop
  - **Example:** Car slowly approaching a person; viewer stays to see if they get hit
  - **When:** Mid-video retention resets and dopamine ladder stages 3–4

- **Pain Point Agitation (Expected Value)**
  - **Structure:** Name a specific "need-to-have" pain point + promise an immediately actionable solution
  - **Example:** "If you struggle with acne, try these three things"
  - **When:** Conversion Game content — building trust with active buyers

- **Shock Value Hook (The Awareness Play)**
  - **Structure:** Extreme or counter-intuitive premise targeting a large Total Addressable Market
  - **Example:** "This is why you should always wash your bread before eating it"
  - **When:** Awareness Game only — max views, broad reach, top of funnel

- **The 6th-Grade Clarity Hook**
  - **Structure:** Simple words + short sentences + direct active voice (Subject + Verb + Object)
  - **Example:** "These guys built a $30 million empire and their secret was their insane journaling practice"
  - **When:** Any hook — eliminates comprehension loss where a viewer bounces because they don't understand

## Storytelling Frameworks

### The Dopamine Ladder (Core System)
Six stages that get viewers addicted by releasing increasing dopamine:

1. **Stimulation (1–2 sec):** Visual stun gun — bright colors, high motion, contrast. Triggers subconscious "bottoms-up" processing.
2. **Captivation:** Open a curiosity loop — pose a question (explicit or implied) the viewer's brain must solve.
3. **Anticipation:** Give details that help viewer guess the answer. Use misdirection/head fakes to reset the loop.
4. **Validation:** Close the loop — reveal the answer or promised value. Must be non-obvious to feel satisfying.
5. **Affection:** Viewer begins to like and trust the creator's personality — buys them a "longer leash."
6. **Revelation:** Viewer realises creator is a consistent source of value → Pavlovian effect: just seeing the creator's face triggers dopamine.

**Psychological principle:** Classical conditioning. Brain releases dopamine while solving open questions.
**Use:** Every video. This is the retention architecture.

### The Curiosity Loop (A vs. B Contrast)
- Identify Point A: viewer's current belief or baseline
- Introduce Point B: contrarian or faster/better alternative
- Highlight the distance: the gap between A and B IS the curiosity loop
- **Principle:** Brain seeks to resolve tension between what it knows and a proposed new reality

### Awareness vs. Conversion Game
Two distinct content systems based on goal:

| | Awareness Game | Conversion Game |
|---|---|---|
| Goal | Max views, ad revenue, brand deals | Leads, sales, email list |
| Hook type | Shock value, curiosity, broad TAM | Pain point, problem-solution |
| Shareability | High — emotional payoff | On-target — niche-specific |
| Virality type | Pure virality | On-target virality |

### The TV Network Strategy
1. **Landing Page:** One "front door" account — aesthetics + links
2. **R&D Account:** Experimental, no rules — test new formats
3. **Spun-out Channels:** Once a format works 3+ times, extract into its own vertical channel

**Principle:** Social engineering — test pilots before greenlighting a full series.

### Likability (The X-Factor Formula)
Three elements that build the Affection stage:
1. **Look:** High-quality visual packaging — feel different from the category
2. **Confidence:** Speaking with mastery from actual foundational knowledge
3. **Authentic Interest:** Same on-camera as off — genuine obsession with the topic

## Content Strategy Rules

### Packaging > Content
- Packaging is more important than the video itself: "if they don't click, they don't watch"
- YouTube packaging = thumbnail + title working as a **perceived value loop**
- Psychology flow: **Visual Stun Gun** (thumbnail catches eye) → **Title Value Hunting** (reading for relevance) → **Visual Validation** (thumbnail confirms the promise)

### Algorithm Mechanics
- Algorithm = "matchmaker" whose only goal is to maximise user minutes for ad revenue
- Sampling process: posts are shown to ~200 strangers first; engagement builds a "fit score"
- If fit score is positive → pushed to 2K → 20K → wider audience
- **Rule:** Be militantly focused on one topic per channel — mixing topics confuses the algorithm and causes "view jail"

### Viral Mechanics
- **Virality is a trap** unless carefully aimed at the right audience
- **Pure Virality:** Broad topics, max views, wrong audience, breaks algorithm matching
- **On-Target Virality:** Maximum penetration within a specific buyer slice
- **The Four Horsemen of Awareness:** Shareworthy content + Curiosity-inducing hook + Emotional payoff + Large TAM
- **Super Shares:** Viral loops ignited when another creator shares your content because it gives them "professional credit"

### Named Frameworks
- **Islands vs. Ecosystems:** Treat each platform as a separate island with native content. Platforms nuke reach when you try to push people off-platform.
- **Category Fandom:** Aim to be the #1 favourite creator in a specific niche — not the world's favourite person
- **Trial Reels (Instagram):** ABC test different hooks by showing variations to non-followers without appearing on your main grid
- **World Building:** Create visual consistency across all platforms so fans recognise you natively everywhere

## Personal Brand Architecture

### Positioning: Category Fandom vs. Peak Fandom
- **Peak Fandom (avoid):** Trying to be everyone's #1 favourite creator globally — impossible unless you're Taylor Swift
- **Category Fandom (target):** Become the #1 most valuable person in one specific narrow topic slice
- **Goal:** Occupy a specific "topic folder" in the viewer's brain so they proactively seek you out when the pain point arises

### Differentiation: The Creator Moat
Five elements that create a signature style no one can copy:
1. **Topic Niche** — the specific subject you cover
2. **Visual Format** — package, layout, aesthetic
3. **Storytelling Style** — how you structure and present ideas
4. **Personality and Vibe** — the aura you project through camera
5. **The X-Factor** — your one-of-one unique ability or perspective

**The Great Divergence:** As AI causes generic content to converge on the same winning formats, humans must differentiate through wild experimentation, crazy formats, and "human taste."

### Magnetic Brand: The 4-Step Playbook
1. **Direction:** High value density — on-target, non-obvious, tactically useful
2. **Comprehension:** Dumb it down into bite-sized chunks so viewers actually absorb it
3. **Effectiveness:** Advice must actually work — solving problems creates a reinforcing trust loop
4. **X-Factor (Likability):** Look + Confidence + Authentic Interest

### Audience Matching Rules
- Social media is no longer "social" — it is just **media**
- **Militant discipline:** One topic, one avatar, per channel
- **Active vs. Passive Viewers:** For business, optimise for active viewers with specific pain points looking for tactical solutions
- **On-Target Virality only:** Never chase pure virality — it destroys algorithm matching

### Psychological Principles of Influence
- **Dopamine Ladder:** Move viewers through all six stages every video
- **Pavlovian Effect:** Ultimate goal — train viewer's brain so just seeing your face releases dopamine
- **Sticky Personal Brand:** In an AI-noise world, the brand must be sticky enough that fans proactively search for you

## Pacing, Retention, and Titles

### Retention Rules
- **Speed to Value:** First 1–2 seconds = topic clarity. Zero fluff. Move core topic to the very beginning.
- **Visual Stun Gun:** Vivid colors + motion + contrast in opening seconds — freezes passive scrolling
- **Curiosity Loops:** Open a loop in the hook; the brain hunts for the answer and keeps watching
- **Head Fakes:** Give details → let viewer guess → yank the answer away → reset the loop
- **The 7/10 Quality Floor:** Any content below 7/10 (professional lighting, 4K, proven storytelling) fails automatically — viewer baseline has risen
- **Comprehension Pacing:** Bite-sized chunks + simple words (6th-grade level) + direct active voice

### Title Formulas
- **Perceived Value Loop:** Every title creates either a **Desire Loop** (pain point: money/time/health) or an **Interest Loop** (curiosity about an unanswered question)
- **Desire Loop example:** "How to write a killer script that keeps viewers hooked"
- **The 6-Power-Word Stack:** Subject Clarity + Action + Objective + Contrast + (Optional) Proof + (Optional) Time
  - Example: "If I had to grow from 0 to 100k subs on YouTube again in 5 months, here's how"

### Thumbnail Psychology
**The 3-Step Click Flow:** Visual Stun Gun → Title Value Hunting → Visual Validation

**7 Elements of a Visual Stun Gun:**
1. **Color Science** — High contrast (e.g., orange on blue)
2. **Large Faces** — Recognisable or unknown faces with extreme emotions (shock, surprise)
3. **Compelling Graphics** — Optical illusions, bright designed elements
4. **Large Text/Numbers** — Huge fonts, dollar signs
5. **Red Circles/Arrows** — Explicitly directing the viewer's eye
6. **Aesthetic Imagery** — Cinematic, symmetrical, or soothing visuals
7. **Design-based Collages** — Maximalist layouts with words and numbers around the subject

**The Rule of Three:** Max 3 elements per thumbnail (face + text + one graphic) — must be legible on small mobile screens

**Visual Validation:** Thumbnail should not repeat the title — it should complement and reinforce the promise

**Testing Protocol:** ABC test at least 3 thumbnails per video using native tools or ClickPilot
