**SPOKEN SCRIPT:**

Getting retrieved by AI and getting recommended by AI are two different things. Most people are only paying for the first one.

Here's the setup. I asked an AI assistant to recommend a realtor. Forty prompts, four phrasings, ten runs each, fresh session every time. Twelve thousand agents in that market. Three names carried the entire set.

So I went looking for why. Reviews? The most-recommended agent has a double-digit review count. Agents sitting on four figures never surfaced once. Sales volume? Those numbers live inside MLS portals behind a login. The model has never seen a row of it. Being genuinely the best is stored somewhere the model physically cannot read.

Then the obvious answer: SEO. Rank number one, get retrieved, get recommended. I checked that too. The page ranking number one for "best realtor" in my city gets pulled on almost every run — and the model still doesn't name the agent at the top of it.

That's the part nobody's building for. The assistant fires a search, pulls back eight to twelve pages, and then throws most of what's on them away. Retrieval gets you in the room. It doesn't get you named. Something happens after retrieval that decides which name gets spoken out loud, and one of those three winners has a single-page site — headshot, phone number, nothing else.

Go run your own version. Ten prompts, fresh sessions, your category. Count the names. If yours isn't in there, the problem isn't your ranking.

Ran 40 prompts asking AI to recommend a realtor. Twelve thousand agents in the market, three names took the whole set — and the #1 ranking page got retrieved every time without its agent ever being named. Ranking and being recommended are not the same system.
