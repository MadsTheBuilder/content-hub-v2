### Tier-2 Titles (A/B Risk — Experimental)

---

**Title 6:** Your AI Agent Is Just a Script. Here's What Real Ones Look Like.
Risk: High technical assumption — challenges what the audience has already built, which creates strong resonance but alienates total beginners. Curiosity gap only fires if they've already shipped something and felt the gap.

---

**Title 7:** I Ran 47 AI Agents for 30 Days. This Is What Breaks Everything.
Risk: Post-mortem framing instead of tutorial framing — smaller audience wants lessons-learned over how-to. Number specificity ("47") mirrors #9's "100 Hours Testing" credibility signal but may feel arbitrary without context.

---

**Title 8:** AI Agents in 2025 Have One Problem. Nobody Talks About It.
Risk: Maximum curiosity gap with zero specificity — no tool name, no framework, no outcome stated. Could be a scroll-stopper; could feel like bait. Thumbnail must do all the heavy lifting to signal technical credibility.

---

**Title 9:** Claude Code Designed My Entire AI Agent System in 10 Minutes
Risk: Tool-specific (Claude Code) narrows the audience; "10 minutes" can read as tutorial-lite. Mirrors #20 energy ("changed forever") but more concrete. May outperform in a Claude-native subscriber base; weaker for general developer search.

---

**Title 10:** I Gave Claude an Agent Framework. It Rewrote It From Scratch.
Risk: High intrigue, unclear payoff — audience must infer what "rewrote" means and why that matters. Breakout potential if the story is sharp; underperforms if the hook doesn't pay off in the first 30 seconds.
