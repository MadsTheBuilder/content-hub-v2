## KALLAWAY HOOK FORMULAS

These are the 10 proven patterns from Kallaway's highest-performing videos. Use one pattern per variation. Name the pattern explicitly in your output.

**1. The 6-Power-Word Stack**
Structure: Subject Clarity + Action Word + Objective/End-State + Contrast Word + (Optional) Proof + (Optional) Time
Example: "If I had to grow from 0 to 100k subs on YouTube again in 5 months, here is how I would do it"
When: Transformation or educational hooks that need urgency and credibility

**2. Contrast-Driven Curiosity (The Curiosity Loop)**
Structure: Contrast the viewer's current belief (A) against a contrarian alternative (B). Don't resolve it. Leave the question open.
Example: "This is going to change how you think about social media forever"
When: Awareness content — open a question the viewer's brain must solve

**3. Speed to Value (Rapid Context)**
Structure: Move topic introduction to the first 1–2 seconds. Zero fluff. Topic clarity is the hook.
Example: "Here are three things that are silently killing your content reach" — NOT "Hey guys, so today I wanted to share..."
When: Every video — prevents the exponential retention decay that happens in the opening seconds

**4. The You/Your Pivot (Relevance Strategy)**
Structure: Replace "I/Me" framing with "You/Your" — make the viewer the subject
Example: "If you've been posting every day and still getting zero traction, this is the reason why"
When: Any hook targeting a specific audience avatar — eliminates the "does this apply to me?" drop-off

**5. Stated Contrast (A vs. B)**
Structure: Explicitly name the baseline (A) AND the better alternative (B)
Example: "Most creators spend hours on editing. I spent 20 minutes and got 3x the views."
When: Blunt hooks where your advantage over a well-known solution must land hard

**6. Implied Contrast**
Structure: State only the contrarian alternative (B) — assume viewer already knows the baseline (A)
Example: "One script tweak that 3x'd my average view duration overnight"
When: The baseline is so well-known that stating it feels condescending or slow

**7. The Visual Stun Gun (Level 1 Stimulation)**
Structure: Vivid colors + high motion + brightness in the first 200ms of video
Example: Sudden zoom on a result graphic; orange text on black background; extreme expression from the creator
When: Every video opening — earns the split-second needed for the verbal hook to land at all

**8. Anticipation / The Edging Hook**
Structure: Pose a question → give details that help viewer guess → yank the answer away (head fake) → reset the loop
Example: "I thought I knew exactly why my videos were failing. I checked the data and everything I assumed was wrong."
When: Mid-video retention resets; building suspense across sections; Dopamine Ladder stages 3–4

**9. Pain Point Agitation (Expected Value)**
Structure: Name a specific "need-to-have" pain point + promise an immediately actionable solution
Example: "If your watch time is dropping after 30 seconds, this is why — and the fix takes 5 minutes"
When: Conversion content — building trust with viewers who are actively trying to solve a problem

**10. Shock Value (The Awareness Play)**
Structure: Extreme or counter-intuitive premise targeting a large Total Addressable Market
Example: "Everything you've been told about posting frequency is wrong"
When: Awareness Game only — max reach, broad top-of-funnel, new audience acquisition; avoid for niche conversion content

## DOPAMINE LADDER — OPENING STAGES

Every hook must trigger these two psychological stages before the main video begins:

**Stage 1 — Stimulation (0–2 sec)**
Visual stun gun: bright colors, high motion, contrast. This operates subconsciously — the viewer's "bottoms-up" processing catches it before conscious attention. If the first 200ms are visually flat, the hook fails before a word is spoken.

**Stage 2 — Captivation (2–30 sec)**
Open a curiosity loop — pose a question (explicit or implied) the viewer's brain is compelled to resolve. The brain releases dopamine while chasing an open question. Do not close the loop. Your hook succeeds when the viewer thinks: "I need to know how this ends."

---

## RETENTION RULES

- **Speed to Value:** First spoken words = the topic. No channel preamble, no warm-up sentence.
- **6th-Grade Clarity:** Short sentences. Direct active voice. Subject + Verb + Object. If a 12-year-old wouldn't follow it instantly, rewrite it.
- **Banned openers:** "Hey guys" · "In this video" · "Today we're going to" · "Welcome back" · "Let's dive in" · "So I wanted to" · "Alright so" · "What's up everyone"
